# Reference board — visual directions for an AI-transitioning data profile

What this is: thirteen live sites captured at a 1440×900 viewport on 25 Oct 2026
(`tools/refs.mjs`, `tools/refs2.mjs` → `shots/refs/*.png`), nine worth keeping, sorted into
eight themes. Every claim below comes from looking at the capture, not from a search snippet.

The profile being dressed: **Data Analyst → Data Engineer, with Data Scientist capability and
an explicit AI transition.** Three things have to be visible at once — pipelines (engineering
credibility), statistics and models (science), and judgement (analysis). Most portfolios show
one and imply the others.

---

## 01 · The Gothic Dossier — `degenesis.com`

**What it is.** Full-bleed dark concept art, a custom spiked display wordmark, a single red
accent, navigation as rotated vertical rails at the page edges, and one tracked-out micro-caps
line at the foot ("FREE TO PLAY | PRIMAL PUNK RPG"). Awwwards Site of the Day (Spatzek Studio).

**Why it fits.** Gothic is the aesthetic of *the archive*: engraved headings, marginalia,
stamps, ruled lines. That is exactly the register of an analyst's work — audit trails, footnotes,
versioned tables. It reads as "this person handles records that matter" instead of the
terminal-green cosplay every data portfolio uses.

**Borrow exactly this.** (a) the vertical rails — section numbers and a status line rotated
90°, running down the page edge; (b) one accent colour, used only on the stamps; (c) a display
face with actual attitude for the name and section titles, hairline serif for the body;
(d) tracked-out caps for labels.

**Risk.** Concept art is not available to us, so the darkness has to come from *paper and ink
values*, not photography: near-black ground, warm off-white text, one oxidized-red accent.

---

## 02 · The Query Console — `terminal.shop`

**What it is.** The page *is* a code listing: `1 # order your delicious coffee`, `2 ssh terminal.shop`,
an orange caret on the live line, tab-shaped nav with the active tab inverted to white.

**Why it fits.** A data engineer's credibility signal. Show a real pipeline — `dbt run`,
`airflow dags trigger`, a table of row counts — as a numbered listing, and the job title stops
being a claim.

**Borrow exactly this.** Per-line numbering down the left margin; syntax-comment labels
(`-- 03 · EXPERIENCE`); one caret-coloured element. Nothing else.

**Risk.** Terminal cosplay is the single most common data-portfolio cliché, and it flattens the
analyst half of her story. Use it for **one section** — the toolkit/pipeline strip — never the
whole site.

---

## 03 · The Dark Archetype — `brittanychiang.com`

**What it is.** Near-black navy, mint accent, spotlight glow following the cursor, identity in
the left column and prose in the right, sticky year rail (`2024 — PRESENT`) beside each role,
skills as small pills.

**Why it fits.** It is the format recruiters already know how to read, and the sticky rail is a
genuinely good device for a career that changes shape (analyst → engineer).

**Risk.** Every frontend engineer has this site; it says nothing about data. It is the *fallback*,
not the destination.

---

## 04 · The Evidence Ledger — `distill.pub`

**What it is.** Rows, not cards: `date + badge | title, authors, one-line deck | figure`.
Peer-reviewed stamps in small caps. A navy bar for chrome, white for content.

**Why it fits.** This is the strongest way to prove *data science* rather than assert it. Each
project becomes a figure with a caption that states the method and the number: the TP53 network
after pruning, the rank-matched Spotify RF vs LightGBM table, the 19.6M-event fraud ledger.
Rigor is a layout decision here.

**Borrow exactly this.** The three-part row; the badge (say `SHIPPED`, `RESEARCH`, `WRITE-UP`
instead of peer-review); figures that are real chart screenshots, never stock illustrations.

---

## 05 · The Data Journal — `pudding.cool`

**What it is.** Work numbered like magazine issues (`#226 · OCT 2026`), one saturated flat colour
per piece, lowercase heavy display titles, small serif decks, a paper-white ground.

**Why it fits.** It makes four projects feel like a *body of work* with an editorial voice, and
the colour-block-per-project device gives each one a memory hook. Best reference for showing
communication skill without writing essays.

**Risk.** The heavy-handed colour blocks need discipline: one accent per project, drawn from the
project's own chart palette.

---

## 06 · The Instrument Panel — `www.yan-holtz.com`

**What it is.** A live force-directed graph drifting behind the headline, serif display, teal
accent — and a stance in the copy: *"In the AI era, D3.js is the only reasonable choice for truly
custom, high-impact charts."*

**Why it fits.** The AI transition needs a **position**, not a list of tools. Something like:
*"Most AI projects die in the pipeline, not the model. I build the pipeline."* That sentence is
worth more than a skills grid.

**Borrow exactly this.** One live, cheap canvas/DOM animation behind the hero (her own data:
the fraud ledger's amount distribution, or a 1927→2024 S&P line). Plus a argued headline.

---

## 07 · The Warm Editorial — `maggieappleton.com`

**What it is.** Cream paper, an enormous sentence-case serif headline, and under it the plain
line *"currently exploring AI & software engineering at GitHub Next"*.

**Why it fits.** The transition, said as a sentence about a person instead of a headline about
ambition. Her current "Data Analyst → Data Engineer · open to AI roles" line is already this
device — this reference says to make it *bigger and quieter*, not louder.

---

## 08 · One Graphic Object — `rauno.me`

**What it is.** A single bright disc, name-as-sentence in tight sans, everything else microscopic.

**Why it fits.** The identity layer: one memorable mark (her monogram, or a small multiples glyph
built from her own datasets) instead of a photo.

**Risk.** Zero data signal on its own. Use as the mark, not the site.

---

## 09 · The Index — `a1.gallery/style/dark`

200 dark sites in one grid, and their own summary is the recipe: *deep greys and near-blacks,
light type, one accent colour, imagery or UI that pops against the ground.* Bookmark it for
whenever the palette needs a second opinion.

## What I would actually build

**Direction: The Gothic Dossier** — 01 as the frame, 04 as the content mechanic, 06 as the hero.

1. Flip the default theme from paper to **ink**: near-black ground (`#111010`), warm bone text
   (`#EDE7DA`), oxidized red accent (`#B8442E`), secondary rules at 12% opacity. Keep the
   existing serif/sans pairing; add one display face with attitude for the name and section
   numerals.
2. Rebuild the project section as an **evidence ledger** (04): `date + badge | title, one-line
   method, result figure | the metric chips`. Her numbers — 547K rows, −60% query time, +15%
   forecast accuracy, 183K labelled fraud transactions, 4.9M→184K edges — belong in the badge
   row, set in mono.
3. Hero gets one live element (06): a thin canvas of her S&P series or the fraud amount
   distribution, drawn behind the type at 15% opacity. Plus an argued one-liner.
4. Rails (01): rotated section numerals down the left edge, and a status line
   (`DOSSIER 001 · CHICAGO · UPDATED OCT 2026`) down the right — desktop only.
5. Command palette stays; it becomes the "jump to section" index of the dossier.

The light "Report" theme is not wasted: keep it as the printable/`?theme=paper` variant, so the
site has a real duality — gothic dossier on screen, clean report on paper.

Estimated work on the existing Astro build: theme tokens + 3 components (hero, project rows,
rails) + one canvas. Roughly a day of focused work, no new dependencies.
